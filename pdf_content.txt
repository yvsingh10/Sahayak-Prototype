1. CLARITY OF PROBLEM DEFINITION (PO1)
1.1 Background
In many areas, public problems such as damaged roads, garbage, or open drains stay unresolved
for a long time. One reason for this is poor communication between citizens and the department
responsible for fixing the issue. Citizens do not have one common platform where they can raise
such problems, so they often depend on informal or unofficial ways of complaining, such as calling
an office directly or asking a local representative.
Because there is no proper system to track a complaint after it is raised, many complaints are
simply forgotten. There is no fixed person responsible and no deadline for action, so problems can
remain pending for a long time without any update.
1.2 Problem Definition
ΓÇó
Public issues such as road damage, pollution, poor sanitation, and local crime are often left
unattended.
ΓÇó
Complaints get delayed because of middlemen and unclear responsibility between
departments.
ΓÇó
There is no fixed time limit for reviewing, approving, or prioritizing a reported problem.
1.3 Key Issues Identified
ΓÇó
There is no transparency in how a reported problem is handled after it is submitted.
ΓÇó
There is no system to separate urgent problems from minor ones.
ΓÇó
Approval of complaints gets delayed due to unclear ownership among authorities.
ΓÇó
Citizens have no way of checking the current status of their complaint.
1.4 Need for a Centralized Platform
Based on the problems listed above, there is a clear need for a single digital platform where
citizens can report public issues and also track what happens to them afterwards. Such a platform
should:
ΓÇó
Give citizens one trusted place to report a problem, instead of relying on multiple informal
channels.
ΓÇó
Make sure every complaint is looked at within a fixed time period, such as 48 hours.
ΓÇó
Allow problems with more public support to be treated with higher priority.
ΓÇó
Help build trust between citizens and the government by showing that action is actually
being taken.

2. DESCRIPTION OF PROJECT CONCEPT (PO4)
2.1 Project Definition
SAHAYAK is a prototype of a digital platform that allows citizens to report public and civic
problems and track their status until they are resolved. It is meant to work as a direct channel
between citizens and the concerned government authority, instead of relying on informal
complaints or word of mouth.
The main users of the platform are citizens, who report problems, and government authorities
such as municipal offices, police, or local representatives, who review and act on these reports.

moment it is reported to the moment it is resolved. Every problem submitted on the platform goes

3. STUDY OF THE LITERATURE SURVEY (PO5)
3.1 Comparable Platforms Studied
Before designing SAHAYAK, a few existing complaint and grievance reporting systems were
studied to understand how they work and what could be learned from them.
CPGRAMS (Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System): This is a
Government of India platform that allows citizens to file grievances against government
departments and track their status online. Studying it gave an idea of how a government-run
complaint tracking system is generally structured.
FixMyStreet: This is a UK-based platform that lets citizens report local issues such as potholes,
broken street lights, or littering by marking the location on a map. This platform was useful in
understanding how location and image-based reporting can be used for civic issues.
MyGov / Swachhata App: These are examples of Indian government platforms that allow
citizens to report civic issues such as garbage or sanitation problems in their area, which are then
forwarded to the concerned department.
3.2 Relevance to SAHAYAK
From studying these platforms, a few useful concepts were identified and adapted for SAHAYAK:
ΓÇó
Reporting a problem along with an image and location, similar to FixMyStreet.
ΓÇó
Routing a complaint to the correct authority or department, similar to CPGRAMS.
ΓÇó
Allowing the public to see the status of a complaint, instead of it being handled only
internally.
However, most of the platforms studied did not have a strict time-bound approval system or a
community-based priority mechanism. This is why these two features were specifically added to
SAHAYAK.

4. PROJECT FEASIBILITY (PO2)
4.1 Technical Feasibility
SAHAYAK can be developed as a web-based platform with a database to store problem reports,
their status, and priority. Since the core functionality mainly involves form submission (description,
image, location), status updates, and basic approval or escalation logic, it does not require any
complex or resource-heavy technology. This makes the prototype realistic to build within the
scope of a college project.
4.2 Operational Feasibility
The platform is designed to fit into how citizens and local authorities already handle complaints,
just in a more structured way. Citizens only need to fill a simple form to report a problem, and
authorities only need to review, approve, and update its status. Since the roles of both citizens
and authorities are kept simple, the platform is operationally practical to use without needing
major training.
4.3 Future Feasibility
Beyond the current prototype, the following enhancements can realistically be added in the future:
ΓÇó
AI-based automatic categorization of complaints.
ΓÇó
Regional language support and voice-based reporting.
ΓÇó
Integration with existing official government portals.
ΓÇó
Performance dashboards for authorities to track resolution rates.
ΓÇó
A penalty system for departments that repeatedly cause delays.
These additions are not part of the current prototype but are realistic directions for extending the
project in the future.

CONCLUSION
SAHAYAK is a prototype that tries to solve a simple but common problem: the lack of a proper
system for reporting and tracking public issues. By introducing a fixed approval time, a priority
system, and visible status tracking, it aims to make the process of reporting a civic problem more
transparent and accountable.
Some of the expected benefits of the system are:
ΓÇó
Faster acknowledgment of public problems.
ΓÇó
More transparency in how complaints are handled.
ΓÇó
Greater accountability from the authorities involved.
ΓÇó
Citizens getting a say in which problems are treated as urgent.
Overall, SAHAYAK is meant to work as a small step toward a more organised and citizen-friendly
way of handling public grievances.
